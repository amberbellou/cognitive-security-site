About & Methods

What this resource is, how every claim in it is verified, and how to use it.

What this is

A curated, verified research collection on cognitive security for AI — the protection of human cognition (belief formation, trust calibration, cognitive capability, emotional autonomy) in interaction with AI systems. It holds 106 papers across 21 topics, organized into five threat classes (T1 belief attacks · T2 capability erosion · T3 trust miscalibration · T4 emotional exploitation · T5 cognitive warfare), with a cross-paper synthesis and a verbatim-retrieval search tool.

The verification guarantees

Every layer is machine-checked by a seven-section QA battery that must fully pass before publication:

  • Title proof — all 106 catalog titles are proven to appear verbatim in their source PDFs (normalized string match, immune to typesetting artifacts).
  • Quote proof — all 161 quotes shown on the Threat Map and in search results are mechanically verified to exist on their cited PDF page. Fabricated quotes cannot pass this check; what remains human is interpretation, not evidence.
  • Consistency — the catalog, analysis records, chatbot corpora, BibTeX, and dataset exports are checked against each other on every run; staleness anywhere fails the build.
  • Ingestion controls — new PDFs pass a scholarly-structure and topic-relevance gate; duplicates (by content hash or title) are automatically excluded.

Curation policy

Selection is by relevance and rigor, never citation count. Cognitive security for AI is a young field whose defining work currently lives in preprints and workshop papers; the collection deliberately spans the full evidence spectrum, and every paper card carries a tier chip so no claim bears more argumentative weight than its evidence supports: 8 Classics (1,000+ citations) · 28 Established (100+) · 25 Cited (10+) · 27 Frontier (recent work, citations pending) · 5 Doctrine (institutional reports: NATO, RAND, industry). Tiers come from an OpenAlex citation audit and are descriptive, not gatekeeping.

How to use it

  • Browse the Paper Index (search-as-you-type, topic jump chips) or the Threat Map (evidence organized by threat mechanism, with verified quotes).
  • Cite — download refs.bib (106 entries, 98 with DOIs; imports into Zotero/Overleaf; cite keys match Threat Map anchors).
  • Interrogate — Ask the Evidence returns exact passages with page citations; no generative AI writes answers, so nothing can be fabricated.
  • Compute — CSV and JSON dataset exports.
  • Read PDFs — paper links open the source repository; access is per-person via GitHub collaborator invitation (copyrighted PDFs are deliberately not public).

Boundaries stated plainly

  • Quote verification proves quotes are real; claim summaries, taxonomy placement, and the synthesis are interpretive scholarship, open to review.
  • The search tool retrieves and cites; it does not explain or summarize.
  • Auto-extracted metadata on newly added papers is provisional until the audit pass.
  • Peer-review status cannot be machine-verified; the collection deliberately includes preprints and institutional reports, labeled as such.
