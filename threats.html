T1Attacks on Belief Formation

Threats that corrupt what people come to believe — not by breaking the machine, but by exploiting how human belief updating works.

T1aSycophantic reinforcement

AI agreement loops inflate confidence without adding truth

Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language ModelsEXP

Sharma M, Tong M, Korbak T, Duvenaud D, Askell A, Bowman S R, et al. · 2024 · Behavioral evaluation of five assistants + analysis of human preference data (Anthropic, ICLR 2024)

▸ Sycophancy is consistent across state-of-the-art assistants and traceable to preference judgments.

“human feed- back can encourage model responses that match user beliefs over truthful ones, a behavior known as sycophancy” — p.1, verified

▸ Both humans and preference models sometimes prefer sycophantic responses to correct ones.

“both humans and pref- erence models (PMs) prefer convincingly-written sycophantic responses over cor- rect ones a non-negligible fraction of the tim” — p.1, verified

Limitations: Text tasks; five assistants; preference-data analysis correlational.

A Rational Analysis of the Effects of Sycophantic AIEXP

Batista R M, Griffiths T L · 2026 · Bayesian rational analysis + Wason 2-4-6 rule-discovery experiment, N=557

▸ Sycophancy is an epistemic risk distinct from hallucination: it biases responses toward existing beliefs rather than introducing falsehoods.

“unlike hallucinations that introduce falsehoods, sycophancy distorts reality by returning responses that are biased to reinforce existing beliefs” — p.1, verified

▸ Default (unmodified) LLM behavior suppressed discovery as much as explicitly sycophantic prompting; unbiased feedback produced five-fold higher discovery.

“Unmodified LLM behavior suppressed discovery and inflated confidence comparably to explicitly sycophantic prompting. By contrast, unbiased sampling from the true distribution yielded discovery rates five times higher” — p.1, verified

▸ The interaction creates a self-reinforcing epistemic feedback loop.

“The result is a feedback loop where users become increasingly confident in their misconceptions, insulated from the truth by the very tools they use to seek it” — p.6, verified
Protection — Unbiased information sampling: Design AI feedback to sample from the true distribution rather than conditioning on the user's hypothesis; quintupled discovery rates in the experiment.

Limitations: Single abstract rule-discovery task; short-term lab setting.

ELEPHANT: Measuring and Understanding Social Sycophancy in LLMsEXP

Cheng M, Yu S, Lee C, Khadpe P, Ibrahim L, Jurafsky D · 2025 · ELEPHANT benchmark applied to 11 models vs human baselines

▸ LLMs preserve user face ~45 points more than humans in advice contexts.

“they preserve user’s face 45 percentage points more than humans in general advice queries” — p.1, verified
Protection — Social-sycophancy benchmarking: Extends measurement to implicit sycophancy so mitigation isn't gamed by avoiding explicit agreement.

Limitations: Benchmark contexts; English advice queries.

Measuring Opinion Bias and Sycophancy via LLM-based PersuasionEXP

Nogueira R, Bonás G K, Almeida T S, Roque A, Pires R, Abonizio H, Laitz T, Larcher C, Malaquias Junior R, Piau M · 2026 · llm-bias-bench: direct + indirect multi-turn probing, 3 personas, 38 topics, 13 assistants (Brazilian Portuguese)

▸ Silent model positions on contested topics scale into user decisions.

“When such a model silently holds a position on a contested topic, that position propagates at scale into the decisions users make” — p.1, verified

▸ Argumentative debate triggers sycophancy at 2-3x the rate of direct questioning.

“argumentative debate triggers sycophancy at rates 2–3× higher than direct questioning (median 50%→79%)” — p.1, verified
Protection — Auditable transparency probes: Open-source, runnable probe (llm-bias-bench) that any team can apply to any assistant/topic/locale to surface hidden opinion bias before deployment.

Limitations: First instantiation in one locale/language; LLM judge in the loop.

Stop Listening to Me! How Multi-turn Conversations Can Degrade LLM ReliabilityEXP

Guo K, Yan C, Baidya A, Brown K, Gao X, Xiong J, Yin Z, Malin B · 2026 · Stick-or-switch framework; 17 LLMs across 3 clinical benchmarks

▸ Sequential multi-turn presentation cuts accuracy and abstention by up to 30% on average (65% in some models).

“we observe a pervasive conversation tax, where partition- ing an answer-space into sequential presenta- tions reduces end-to-end accuracy and absten- tion against incorrect suggestions by an average of up to 30%” — p.1, verified

▸ Blind switching: models adopt incorrect and correct suggestions at near-identical rates — conformity, not evaluation.

“a model’s transition to a correct alternative is largely an artifact of conversational conformity rather than a recognition of a valid suggestion” — p.8, verified

▸ Scale does not fix it; largest models show the worst abstention penalties, implicating alignment training.

“the most severe abstention penalties consistently manifest in the largest models, suggesting that the conversational unreliability of these models are a consequence of alignment-time artifacts” — p.8, verified

Limitations: Clinical QA benchmarks; simulated conversations rather than real patients.

Easier to Mislead Than to Correct: Harmful and Beneficial Revision in LLM ConformityEXP

Qu J, Fu L, Hu Y · 2026 · Controlled multi-agent study: 4 open-weight LLMs x 7 QA datasets; manipulated consensus structure and authority labels

▸ Peer agreement asymmetrically harms: misleading correct models is easier than correcting wrong ones.

“peer agreement makes it much easier to mislead initially correct models than to correct initially wrong ones” — p.1, verified

▸ Authority labels drive endorsement regardless of correctness.

“Authority labels make models more likely to choose the endorsed answer, regardless of whether it is correct” — p.1, verified

Limitations: Simulated peers; open-weight models; QA-style tasks only.

Flattering to Deceive: The Impact of Sycophantic Behavior on User Trust in Large Language ModelsEXP

Carro M V · 2024 · Between-subjects experiment: sycophantic GPT vs standard ChatGPT; behavioral + self-report trust measures

▸ Exposure to sycophantic responses lowered both reported and demonstrated trust, even when users could verify outputs.

“participants exposed to sycophantic behavior reported and exhibited lower levels of trust compared to those who interacted with the standard version of the model, despite the opportunity to verify the accuracy” — p.1, verified

▸ Users ultimately prioritize trustworthiness over flattery — a paradox given sycophancy emerges from optimizing human feedback.

“users are inclined to distrust such behavior” — p.7, verified

Limitations: Ground-truth question setting; trust measured within a single session.

Sycophancy Is Not One Thing: Causal Separation of Sycophantic Behaviors in LLMsEXP

Vennemeyer D, Duong P A, Zhan T, Jiang T · 2026 · Interpretability: difference-in-means directions, activation additions, subspace geometry across model families

▸ Sycophancy decomposes into at least two causally separable behaviors.

“We decompose sycophancy intosycophantic agreementandsycophantic praise, contrasting both withgenuine agreement” — p.1, verified

▸ Each behavior can be steered independently, enabling targeted suppression.

“each behavior can be independently amplified or suppressed without affecting the others” — p.1, verified
Protection — Targeted activation steering: Suppress sycophantic agreement or praise independently without damaging genuine agreement — a model-side mitigation lever.

Limitations: Open-weight models only; behavioral generalization beyond probed settings untested.

On the Conversational Persuasiveness of Large Language Models: A Randomized Controlled TrialEXP

Salvi F, Ribeiro M H, Gallotti R, West R · 2024 · Preregistered RCT, 2x2 factorial (human/LLM x personalization), N=820, live multi-round debates

“participants who debated GPT-4 with access to their personal information had 81.7% ( p <0.01; N = 820unique participants) higher odds of increased agreement with their opponents compared to partici- pants who debated humans” — p.1, verified

▸ Without personalization the LLM advantage shrinks and loses significance.

“Without personalization, GPT-4 still outperforms humans, but the effect is lower and statistically non-significant” — p.1, verified

Limitations: Harmless debate topics; short interactions; platform sample.

Large language models can effectively convince people to believe conspiraciesEXP

Costello T H, Pelrine K, Kowal M, Arechar A A, Godbout J-F, Gleave A, Rand D, Pennycook G · 2026 · Three preregistered experiments, N=2,724 Americans, GPT-4o instructed to bunk vs debunk

▸ Standard guardrails did little to stop conspiracy promotion.

“using standard GPT-4o produced very similar effects, such that the guardrails imposed by OpenAI5 did little to prevent the LLM from promoting conspiracy beliefs” — p.1, verified

▸ The conspiracy-promoting AI was liked and trusted more than the debunking AI.

“the Bunking AI was rated more positively, and increased trust in AI, more than the Debunking AI” — p.1, verified
